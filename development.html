Development

From the first build to the launch

Ant Laboratory 1.0.2 is on the App Store, since 15 October 2026. It is the first public release: 1.0.0 and 1.0.1 were development milestones and were never on sale. Here is what each step added, and what is planned next.

  • 1.0.0
  • 1.0.1
  • 1.0.2, on the App Store
  • What’s next

  1. 1.0.0Test builds · September and October 2026

    The first observer build

    The heart of the game: a colony that runs itself, to watch and to study.

    • The ant farm. Six species, one queen in a glass ant farm, and every ant in it simulated, with her own age, job and history. An automatic feeder, food to offer by hand, and the Ant cam to follow one ant close up.
    • The island. Open the access port, and the colony spreads across an island grown from a seed: outdoor nests linked by trails, rival colonies that fight along the borders, scouts and outposts, and a cut-away view inside every nest.
    • A changing world. Seasons with snow and frozen ponds, and storms, droughts, wildfire, spiders and disease out on the land. The ant farm stays sealed.
    • The Lab. Field notes and charts of the colony’s life, a table for every chart, and the records as spreadsheet files.
    • At your pace. Pause, or speed up to 25×. Cinematic mode to sit back and watch, and several colonies, each on its own island. Quiet nature sounds made by the game as it plays, and no music.

  2. 1.0.1Build 11 · 3 October 2026

    A living ant farm

    The glass ant farm became a small living habitat that you look after.

    • Habitat. Set the farm’s light, water and warmth. Plants follow the lamp, mould grows where it is damp, tiny springtails graze it, and over weeks the bare surface turns green. Keep the soil too dry or too wet for days, and some brood dies.
    • The lodge and its radio. Pinch out to see the whole room around the farm: a computer that shows the colony’s readings, and a radio that plays the developer’s own soundtrack, four calm tracks, off until you tap it.
    • Data. The Lab became Data, with every chart for the ant farm, the outdoor nests or the whole colony, and new charts for the habitat.
    • Truer to life. The ants widen crowded rooms before they dig new ones, as real ants do. Only a real successor can take over from a queen, so a colony can now die out.

  3. 1.0.2Build 13 · On the App Store, 15 October 2026

    The first public release

    Version 1.0.2 brings together everything above, and this. It is on the App Store, US$4.99, paid once.

    • Recorded nature sounds. Wind, rain, insects, water, fire, thunder and some of the birds are recordings the developer made with ElevenLabs, quiet and changing with the place, the season and the weather. The ants’ sounds are still made by the game.
    • Weather you call. On the island, strike lightning or call a downpour where you tap. Every bolt and downpour is noted in Field notes, and the ant farm stays sealed.
    • A colony that fills its tank. Kept contained, a colony grows on for years and digs across the whole farm, up to about 4,500 ants and brood.
    • Choose the soil. Six soils for a new colony’s farm, as you would choose for a real tank. Only the look changes.
    • A brighter lamp and a loudspeaker. The lamp goes up to twice as bright. A loudspeaker on the lodge’s outside wall plays the radio out on the island, when you turn it on.
    • The map and the nest agree. A mound looks the same from above and from the side: its size, its entrances, its snow and fallen leaves.
    • The dead by the nest. In the ant farm, the dead are usually laid on a small refuse heap by the entrance, as real ants do.
    • New art. The food, mushrooms, aphids and springtails have new pictures.
    • iPhone Duo. Designed for its tabletop mode: half open on a table, the colony fills the top half, with the controls and a live dashboard below the fold. See it on a table.

How it is made

Ant Laboratory is made by one developer, Jahoward Hutchins, over months of design, research and testing on real iPhones and iPads. I set the vision and the design, decide how the game plays, direct every piece of art and test every build myself.

I build it with AI tools, and I want to be open about how. Claude Code, Anthropic’s AI coding assistant, helps write the code and the game’s text, gathers and reads the biology research and runs the tests, and separate Claude reviewers check each piece of work before it ships. The radio’s music was made with Suno and ElevenLabs, and many of the nature sounds with ElevenLabs. Every species is drawn from real measurements, and every change is tested before it reaches you.

The science is checked as carefully as the code. Every behaviour is researched from published studies before it is built, and more than 100 automated tests play the game after every change: they check that the simulation still grows the same colony from the same seed, that the ant farm stays sealed and that saved colonies always open. The simulation follows real biology in what happens, but its rates are sped up and simplified, so it is a way to explore ants, not a scientific instrument. More on The science.

Ant Laboratory is an independent game, not made or endorsed by Anthropic.

What’s next

Plans, not promises. These are ideas being worked on. Any of them may change, come later or be left out, and none of them is in the game yet.

  1. 1.0.3Planned · the next update

    A more real formicarium

    • Closing the access port. Close the port and open it again whenever you like, like a valve. A card first says plainly what closing costs: outdoor nests cut off from the queen raise no new young, and slowly die out over months, as they would in nature.
    • Winged ants in a closed tank. In a sealed tank, winged queens and males cannot fly away. In the mating season they gather under the lid, and you choose: release them, or keep them in.
    • Keeping, as real keepers do. The ants tidy mouldy food away to the refuse heap. A keeper’s notebook marks your changes on the charts. A health card says what is holding the colony back. Each species keeps its own hours on the map, and old refuse heaps turn into rich soil that greens over.

  2. 2.0Planned · a big update

    The living island

    • Daughter colonies. Each summer’s mating flight sends out winged queens, and a few found colonies of their own. Over the years the colony’s line spreads across the island, with borders and wars between its colonies, even between relatives.
    • Supercolonies. Wood ants take daughter queens back in and bud new nests, growing into one connected colony across the island, as real wood ant supercolonies do.
    • Planting. Plant trees and shrubs: aphids live on them, and their honeydew feeds more ants.
    • Nests that read as real nests. Rooms you can tell apart by what is in them, and tunnels that look carved.

  3. LaterAn idea for 3.0 · not designed yet

    Termites

    Termites and ants are old enemies. Many ants hunt termites, and termites defend themselves with soldiers, sticky sprays and walled-off tunnels. They could come to the island as a new kind of neighbour: prized but costly prey, rivals for dead wood, and swarms of winged termites after the first warm rains. With them could come a new species, the Matabele ant, which raids termites in columns on the African savanna and carries its injured home. Like the island’s other dangers, termites would live outdoors only, never in the ant farm.
