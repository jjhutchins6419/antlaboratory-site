Development

From the first build to today

Ant Laboratory 1.0.2, the first public release, came to the App Store on 15 October 2026; 1.0.0 and 1.0.1 were development milestones and were never on sale. 2.0, The Naturalist, the first update, followed on 19 October 2026, and the Android beta has begun on Google Play. Here is what each step added, and what is planned next.

  1. 1.0.0Test builds · September and October 2026

    The first observer build

    The heart of the game: a colony that runs itself, to watch and to study.

    • The ant farm. Six species, one queen in a glass ant farm, and every ant in it simulated, with her own age, job and history. An automatic feeder, food to offer by hand, and the Ant cam to follow one ant close up.
    • The island. Open the access port, and the colony spreads across an island grown from a seed: outdoor nests linked by trails, rival colonies that fight along the borders, scouts and outposts, and a cut-away view inside every nest.
    • A changing world. Seasons with snow and frozen ponds, and storms, droughts, wildfire, spiders and disease out on the land. The ant farm stays sealed.
    • The Lab. Field notes and charts of the colony’s life, a table for every chart, and the records as spreadsheet files.
    • At your pace. Pause, or speed up to 25×. Cinematic mode to sit back and watch, and several colonies, each on its own island. Quiet nature sounds made by the game as it plays, and no music.

  2. 1.0.1Build 11 · 3 October 2026

    A living ant farm

    The glass ant farm became a small living habitat that you look after.

    • Habitat. Set the farm’s light, water and warmth. Plants follow the lamp, mould grows where it is damp, tiny springtails graze it, and over weeks the bare surface turns green. Keep the soil too dry or too wet for days, and some brood dies.
    • The lodge and its radio. Pinch out to see the whole room around the farm: a computer that shows the colony’s readings, and a radio that plays the developer’s own soundtrack, four calm tracks, off until you tap it.
    • Data. The Lab became Data, with every chart for the ant farm, the outdoor nests or the whole colony, and new charts for the habitat.
    • Truer to life. The ants widen crowded rooms before they dig new ones, as real ants do. Only a real successor can take over from a queen, so a colony can now die out.

  3. 1.0.2Build 13 · On the App Store, 15 October 2026

    The first public release

    Version 1.0.2 brings together everything above, and this. It went on sale on the App Store at US$4.99, paid once.

    • Recorded nature sounds. Wind, rain, insects, water, fire, thunder and some of the birds are recordings the developer made, quiet and changing with the place, the season and the weather. The ants’ sounds are still made by the game.
    • Weather you call. On the island, strike lightning or call a downpour where you tap. Every bolt and downpour is noted in Field notes, and the ant farm stays sealed.
    • A colony that fills its tank. Kept contained, a colony grows on for years and digs across the whole farm, up to about 4,500 ants and brood.
    • Choose the soil. Six soils for a new colony’s farm, as you would choose for a real tank. Only the look changes.
    • A brighter lamp and a loudspeaker. The lamp goes up to twice as bright. A loudspeaker on the lodge’s outside wall plays the radio out on the island, when you turn it on.
    • The map and the nest agree. A mound looks the same from above and from the side: its size, its entrances, its snow and fallen leaves.
    • The dead by the nest. In the ant farm, the dead are usually laid on a small refuse heap by the entrance, as real ants do.
    • New art. The food, mushrooms, aphids and springtails have new pictures.
    • iPhone Duo. Works with its tabletop mode: half open on a table, the colony fills the top half, with the controls and a live dashboard below the fold. See it on a table.

  4. 2.0Build 17 · On the App Store, 19 October 2026

    The Naturalist

    The first update, and the biggest yet. Everything in it.

    • A new look. Clear glass controls in forest green and brass, or the Classic look if you prefer it. One sun lights the farm, the nests and the island: richer soil, carved tunnels, soft shadows, new plants and trees, and ants on the floors, walls and glass of their rooms.
    • Water and light. A smooth coast with foam, the sea by depth, ponds and streams that look and flow like water, and a slow day and night on the island, with stars and the moon.
    • The lodge comes alive. The lodge at its real size, a well-kept field station with windows onto the island’s own land in every season, things to tap, and eight tracks on the radio.
    • Look closer. Examine an ant, lift her into a dish under the lens, and read the field guide, with each species drawn at true size. A soft silver patch marks the ant you follow.
    • A more real formicarium. A Clean tool that lifts out the dead, mist by hand, a warm end and a damp end, a winter rest, spoilt food tidied away, rich soil from old refuse heaps, and a keeper’s notebook and health card in Data.
    • The access port. Close it again whenever you like, and choose whether a closed farm’s winged ants fly.
    • The living island. Daughter colonies from the mating flight, one supercolony for wood ants, planting trees and shrubs, burnt land coming back richer and young trees growing back over years.

How it is made

Ant Laboratory is made by one independent developer over months of design, research and testing on real iPhones and iPads. I set the vision and the design, decide how the game plays, direct every piece of art and test every build myself.

The science is checked as carefully as the code. Every behaviour is researched from published studies before it is built, and more than 100 automated tests play the game after every change: they check that the simulation still grows the same colony from the same seed, that the ant farm stays sealed and that saved colonies always open. The simulation follows real biology in what happens, but its rates are sped up and simplified, so it is a way to explore ants, not a scientific instrument. More on The science.

  2. 2.1Planned · the next update

    The laboratory update

    • A queen in a test tube. Your new queen arrives in a test tube, as real ant keepers get theirs, and you release her into her new home yourself.
    • Walk round the research station. See the whole room: a formicarium wall for your tanks, and a gallery wall for photographs of your ants.
    • More real species. Turtle ants are the next real species to join the six, and more will follow, including small colonies that you look after up close.
    • Live food. Feed fruit flies, small crickets, mealworms and waxworms, and watch the ants hunt them down.
    • Real glass and new formicariums. Real glass on the tanks, and new formicarium designs: tall towers, wide display tanks, and sets of tanks that join together with tubes.
    • Weather and seasons you set. Choose the island’s weather and seasons. Keep it hot for a long time and the land slowly turns to desert; keep it cold and it turns to taiga, the cold northern forest.
    • Softer light and sound. Gentler light, and gentler sound, all through the game.

  3. LaterAfter 2.1

    Hive Mind

    A second way to play, for when you want to lead. In Hive Mind you choose where new nests go, send out scouts and lead your colony against its rivals, and a careless leader can lose nests. Observer, the way the game plays now, stays just as it is.

  4. LaterAn idea · not designed yet

    Termites

    Termites and ants are old enemies. Many ants hunt termites, and termites defend themselves with soldiers, sticky sprays and walled-off tunnels. They could come to the island as a new kind of neighbour: prized but costly prey, rivals for dead wood, and swarms of winged termites after the first warm rains. With them could come another real species, the Matabele ant, which raids termites in columns on the African savanna and carries its injured home. Like the island’s other dangers, termites would live outdoors only, never in the ant farm.
