The ant farm

A colony that runs itself

Begin with a queen in the formicarium. Watch the first workers emerge, then the colony takes over. Founding is simplified for the sandbox; the wood ant’s solo start is marked Experimental. Nurses feed the larvae, diggers carve tunnels and chambers, foragers lay scent trails to food and carry it home, and undertakers take away the dead.

Every ant in the farm is simulated, with her own age, job and history. Tap one to see what she is doing and why, or touch and hold to follow her through the nest.

The Ant cam

A small camera follows one ant close up and says where she is going, what she carries and why she does her job. It takes turns between the queen and busy workers, or stays with any ant you tap. Tap it to watch her full screen.

The island

Then it spreads across an island of its own

As the farm fills, choose whether to open its access port. You can keep the colony contained and open it later from Colony or More. Once open, the farm stays connected to the island: the founding queen remains indoors while workers travel between the farm and outdoor nests. Trails link the nests, rivals press at the borders, and the colony spreads through woodland, meadow, heath, dunes and wetland.

Streams run down to the sea and ponds lie in the hollows. The water moves and catches the light, rain rings its surface, and storms flood the low ground around it.

A new island for every colony

Each island is grown from a seed: its size and coastline, hills, streams and ponds, woods and open country, the ecosystems in its regions, and the names of its regions and landmarks. Copy the seed from the island's card on the map, and anyone who starts the same species and size with it grows the same island.

Underground

Dive inside any nest

Pinch into a nest on the map and the view dives underground: its chambers, brood and food stores, and the workers moving between them. Each species has its own nest architecture. Outdoor ants are a representative visual sample of that nest’s activity; the farm simulates its ants individually.

Rival colonies build in their own ways: thatch mounds, fungus gardens, seed granaries, galleries in wood.

The seasons

The island changes through the year

A year passes in a little over an hour at 1×. The land, the view from the shelter's windows and the sounds all follow it.

Spring

Fresh leaves and new grass, and blossom on the flowering trees. Birds sing a dawn chorus, and resting colonies wake up.

Summer

Deep greens, then dry grass bleaching to straw late in the summer. Crickets sing, and big colonies send out winged queens and males.

Autumn

The leaves turn gold, orange and red, then fall and lie on the ground. Dry leaves rustle, the wind gusts and geese fly over.

Winter

Bare trees. In a cold winter snow settles on the land and the mounds, and the ponds freeze. Most species rest deep in the nest until spring.

Winter follows the island's climate: snow and ice where it is cool, a wet grey winter where it is warm or dry, and none in the tropics.

A wild world

Wildfires, storms and recovery

Out on the land, lightning kills where it strikes and sets dry ground alight, and the wind drives the fire on until it meets a stream, a track or rain. The burnt ground turns black, then grows back greener than before. Thunderstorms flood the low ground and fill nests from the bottom up, and droughts wither the plants. Wolf spiders hunt at the nest entrances, and fungal disease spreads on damp ground. The ant farm stays a clean, closed enclosure: the colony's safe heart.

Watch these natural events unfold and follow their effects in the Lab. The indoor formicarium stays protected from outdoor threats.

The Lab

Study it like a scientist

The Lab opens to Field notes, with measured changes to explore at your own pace. There are no required research tasks. It measures the colony every day: population and brood, births and deaths by cause, lifespans and survival, who does which job, food and diet, soil temperature, ecology and territory. Once the colony lives outdoors too, each section starts with the whole colony, then shows the founding farm ant by ant. Every chart has a table view, and the data exports as spreadsheet files for your own analysis.

The biology is real where it can be. The rates are sped up so a colony's life fits into hours of play, and anything beyond real biology is marked Experimental.

Observation 1.0

Watch, inspect, investigate

Observer

Relax and watch. The feeder keeps the colony fed, and it makes its own decisions.

Inspect

Follow a worker, look inside a chamber and see where food goes. Pause for a closer look whenever something catches your eye.

Investigate

Start with Field notes, compare the charts and export the records. Look for changes in growth, food or temperature, then watch what follows.

Your pace

At 1× a colony day passes in 12 seconds; go up to 25× faster, or pause. When you leave, it waits right where you left it.

Sounds, not music

Wind in the trees, birds that change with the seasons, rain, shifting soil and scraping ants, made by the game as it plays. Bring your own music.

Cinematic mode

Hold your chosen view or tour the formicarium. Once the access port is open, tour the whole colony too. Optional summaries describe what is happening. Touch the screen to return to your controls.

Feed the island

Scatter seeds, sugar, flies or crickets by an outdoor nest: sugar ends hunger, insects feed the brood. The nests live off their land, so it is extra help, never a chore.

A view to share

Cinematic hides the controls and labels. Leave its summaries off to show just the living world, with a fixed view or a moving camera.

Home and your settings

Resume, start a new colony or open your saves from Home. Choose an interface theme without changing the scenery. A Bright, Soft or Off lamp lights the indoor farm.

Its own story

When the colony is full-grown, it tells its story so far: its queens, its growth and its firsts. The Lab keeps it for you to read again.

Many colonies

Start as many as you like, each on its own island, and switch between them. Export one to keep or share. Delete one, and a recovery copy stays in case you change your mind.

Six species

Each with its own nest, diet and habits

  • Black garden antLasius nigerFarms aphids for honeydew at a garden's edge.
  • Fire antSolenopsis invictaSmall, fierce and fast-growing; in a flood the workers form rafts.
  • Wood antFormica rufaBuilds a thatch mound; colonies may have one queen or several.
  • Leafcutter antAtta sexdensCuts leaves to grow a fungus garden, from tiny gardeners to big soldiers.
  • Carpenter antCamponotus pennsylvanicusCarves its galleries in rotting wood; big, slow and patient.
  • Harvester antPogonomyrmex barbatusGathers seeds into granaries on dry grassland.
